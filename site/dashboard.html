How the prediction works

Each prediction looks at the same train's past trips between the same two stations over the last 15 months. Every past trip gets a weight, and the on-time chance is the weighted share of those trips that arrived on time. The likely arrival time and the "8 in 10" range come from the same weighted trips.

  • Recency: a trip's weight halves every 30 days, so the last few weeks count most.
  • Time of year (weather, summer heat, holidays): trips within about a month of the same date in another year get a bonus of up to +1. The bonus falls to about +0.6 for trips a month off and +0.14 for two months off, and halves for each year back, so the same week last year still counts about 0.5.
  • Day of week: the same weekday counts fully (×1), another weekday (or the other weekend day) ×0.7, and a weekday versus a weekend day ×0.49.
  • Time of day is built in, because each train runs at its own scheduled time.
  • Little history: a train's odds are blended with the route's other trains as if they added 10 extra trips, so a train with few trips doesn't swing to 0% or 100%. A train with no history at all gets the route's odds.

Source: Amtrak Status Maps (asm.transitdocs.com), which records Amtrak's reported arrival and departure times. Delays use Amtrak's reported actual times. Amtrak seldom posts an actual arrival at a train's last stop (for example New York for the Crescent), so there the final estimate is used once the train has left the stop before. Trips with no reported time are left out of delay stats. Canceled trips are counted separately.
